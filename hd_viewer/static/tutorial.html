Overview

The start page has four parts, from top to bottom:

  1. Choose your data source: upload your own files or pick an example dataset.
  2. Preprocessing: which representations of the sample to build (pseudospots, cells).
  3. Load data, with a status line below it.
  4. Loading progress: one progress bar per task.

When every task has finished, the status line reads Done and the viewer opens. Everything you do afterwards lives in your browser session: reloading the page brings you back here, and nothing is saved between sessions (except cached segmentation results, see below).

The files you need

One Visium HD sample, as produced by 10x Genomics Space Ranger, at one bin size:

FieldFile in the Space Ranger outputNotes
1. Expression data (.h5) binned_outputs/square_XXXum/filtered_feature_bc_matrix.h5 Counts of every gene in every bin under the tissue.
2. Positions data (.parquet) binned_outputs/square_XXXum/spatial/tissue_positions.parquet Where each bin is on the image. Must come from the same square_XXXum folder as the .h5.
3. Resolution (µm)the XXX of that folder: 2, 8 or 16 Bin size of the two files above.
4. Tissue imagethe full-resolution image given to Space Ranger .btf, .tif/.tiff or .png. Used as the background, to compute physical distances and for cell segmentation.
Which bin size? 2 µm is the native resolution and the largest data (millions of bins; loading takes minutes). 8 and 16 µm load faster. Whatever you choose, the viewer can still sum bins into larger cells while you explore (see bin resolution).

What to compute

Bins (always)

The bins of your files are always loaded. They become the Bins layer of the viewer.

Generate Visium v2 pseudospots

Builds a grid of 55 µm spots like standard Visium (v2) and aggregates the bins under each spot. Useful to compare with standard Visium data, or with each other in the Comparison panel. They become the Spots layer.

Cell segmentation

Each method you tick finds cells (or nuclei) in the tissue image. Then every bin lying entirely inside a cell is assigned to it, and the cell's expression is the sum of its bins. Bins inside more than one cell are left out. Every method becomes its own cells layer (a tab in the Cells section, with its own outline colour), so you can compare methods on the same tissue.

  • Tick several methods to compare them. Two run at the same time; the others wait, shown as Queued.
  • Settings appear when you tick a method. Leaving a field empty uses the method's own default.
  • Results are cached next to the dataset, per image, method version and settings: loading the same sample with the same settings again is instant.
  • Methods run in their own containers (Docker or Apptainer) or Python environments, as configured by whoever runs the server. The first run of a method may download its image.

The methods and their settings

MethodWhat it findsSettings
StarDistNuclei, with a deep-learning model trained on H&E images. Probability / overlap thresholds: lower finds more nuclei (empty = calibrated values). Normalisation percentiles: image contrast stretch. Block size: image tiles processed at once (memory vs. speed).
CellposeWhole cells (cyto3) or nuclei (nuclei). Expected diameter (µm): typical cell size. Flow threshold: higher keeps more, less regular cells. Cell probability threshold: lower finds more and larger cells. Block size.
Watershed (baseline)Nuclei, with classical image processing (no model). Smoothing, Threshold factor (higher keeps fewer, darker nuclei), Min. distance between nuclei, Min. nucleus area, Block size.

Segmenting a whole slide on a CPU takes from minutes (watershed) to much longer (StarDist, Cellpose).

Troubleshooting

SymptomWhat to do
"Please upload …"A required file is missing (see the four fields above).
Bins load but look misplacedThe positions file and the .h5 come from different bin sizes, or the image is not the one given to Space Ranger.
A segmentation bar failsThe bar shows the reason. The full log is in <dataset folder>/segmentation/<method>/<id>.running/run.log, and the error is printed in the server console.
"killed, most likely out of memory"The server ran out of memory for that method; ask the administrator to give it more memory, or try a smaller block size.
"No module named …" / container pull errorsThe method is not installed or its image cannot be downloaded on this server; contact the administrator.

The map

The tissue image is drawn with your layers on top. Scroll to zoom and drag to pan. Only the visible region is loaded, so new areas fill in as you move. Hover an observation to see its values: the gene shown, and its group and cluster when there are any.

The image keeps its original orientation and aspect ratio. Transparent padding supplies the square tile canvas, and bin rectangles follow the tissue grid's rotation. Original image files and annotation coordinates are preserved.

Image alpha, below the map, fades the tissue image so the layers stand out.

Drag the horizontal divider below the map controls to adjust the map's height. Differential-expression results scroll separately in the section below it.

Cells layers are drawn at every zoom; zoomed out, their outlines are simplified to the detail the screen can show.

Measuring distances

Pick the ruler in the toolbar and click two points: a dashed line joins them and its length is shown in µm (or mm). Drag either end to adjust; a third click starts a new measurement. The eraser button next to it clears it.

Bin resolution

The panel at the top right of the map controls how bins are drawn:

  • Dynamic · follow zoom (default): zoomed out, bins are summed into larger cells (2 × 2, 4 × 4, 8 × 8, 12 × 12 bins) so the map stays fast; zooming in shows native bins again.
  • Fixed · …: always draw one level.

Aggregates are blocks of neighbouring array rows and columns, anchored at array index zero. Their membership stays the same as you pan and zoom; incomplete edge blocks use the bins present.

The panel also shows the size of the drawn cells, their rotation and the native bin size. While bin clusters are shown, the level they were computed at is used and the selector is locked. Click − to fold the panel into a single line and + to unfold it.

Overview and coordinates

The overview (top right) shows the whole slide, with a rectangle marking the region the map is showing. Its corner gives the coordinates of that region in full-resolution image pixels (the same system as Space Ranger's pxl_col_in_fullres / pxl_row_in_fullres) and the size of its enclosing bounding box. The coordinates refer to the original image, excluding the canvas padding. The copy button copies x0,y0,x1,y1, e.g. to crop the image elsewhere.

Layers

Each representation of the sample is a layer: Spots (pseudospots), Bins and one Cells layer per segmentation method (tabs inside the Cells section). Every layer has the same tabs. An observation is one spot, bin or cell. Not-sequenced observations (inside the tissue but without counts) are drawn in grey.

General tab

  • Show …: show or hide the whole layer.
  • Show not sequenced …: show or hide the grey observations without counts.
  • … alpha: maximum opacity. When colouring by a gene, opacity follows its expression up to this value; with categories (clusters, groups) every observation uses it.
  • Download (Spots only): the pseudospots as an AnnData file (.h5ad) with their counts, as they were when the viewer opened (clusters and groups added later are not included).

Color tab

  • Gene / Metadata: type at least two letters and pick a gene or a metadata column.
  • For genes and numeric columns, observations get one colour (the colour picker) and their opacity follows the value.
  • For categorical columns (clusters, groups, …), each category gets its own colour. Clusters and groups keep the same colours everywhere: map, lists and DE results. Groups and clusters never share a colour.

Clustering tab

Groups observations by similar expression using the selected algorithm.

  1. Choose Leiden, K-means, DBSCAN, HDBSCAN, Gaussian mixtures or agglomerative clustering. For Leiden, higher resolution gives more, smaller clusters. K-means, mixtures and agglomerative clustering take a number of clusters. DBSCAN takes an epsilon distance in PCA space and minimum samples; HDBSCAN takes minimum cluster size and minimum samples. Mixtures offer covariance types, and agglomerative clustering offers linkage choices.
  2. Click Run clustering. Behind the scenes: normalisation, log transform, highly variable genes, then PCA. Leiden also builds a neighbour graph. Preprocessing and clustering results are cached for reuse with the same parameters.
  3. As soon as clustering finishes, the map is coloured by cluster and the list of clusters appears, each with its number of observations.
  4. Then the marker genes (Wilcoxon test, each cluster vs the rest) are ranked in the background: the top five fill in under each cluster, and the top ten appear in the Summary. Run clustering is available again when they are ready. Density-method outliers appear as Noise. Noise and groups with fewer than two observations have no marker ranking; neither does a single resulting group.

Tick or untick clusters (or use Select all / Deselect all) to show or hide them on the map. For bins, clustering runs at the displayed level and the map stays at that level while the clusters are shown.

Deconvolution tab

Optionally upload a single-cell .h5ad or .h5 reference on the initial loading screen, when uploading your own spatial data. Examples use a configured local reference when available and otherwise run without one; reference upload controls are hidden for all examples. A supplied reference is loaded once and available in every layer's Deconvolution tab. AnnData .obs annotations load automatically, including from files named .h5. The Human CRC example loads the count matrix, SingleCell_MetaData.csv and P1CRC_Metadata.parquet from its local Chromium folder without uploads. The CSV annotates the reference cells; deconvolution defaults to Level1, with Level2 and the other reference columns available in the selector. The Parquet labels spatial bins and is attached to the spatial expression data. Standard 10x .h5 files contain counts without cell-type labels: upload an annotation CSV or Parquet file alongside the reference, with matching cell barcodes in the first column and annotations in the other columns. Parquet may also store barcodes in its index or a column named barcode. These annotation columns are offered in the tab. Choose the cell-type annotation column, then run deconvolution. Both datasets need matching, unique gene identifiers and nonnegative linear expression in .X, not log counts.

Choose NNLS, cell2location, MuSiC, or CIBERSORTx in the method selector. Controls change with the method. NNLS estimates expression mixture fractions from library-normalised reference profiles; these are not calibrated cell counts. cell2location requires an expected cell count per location and offers posterior mean abundance alongside relative fractions. MuSiC requires a donor/sample annotation containing multiple subjects. CIBERSORTx generates a signature from the uploaded reference and requires its official container and server-configured credentials.

Each run analyses all native observations in the layer in the background. Empty observations remain unassigned. Choose a saved run, readout, and cell type to show results on the map. Coarse bins average fractions and sum expected cell abundance. Numbered metadata columns retain every run; the latest fractions remain in obsm['deconvolution'] and their details in uns['deconvolution']. All run metadata is retained in uns['deconvolution_runs'], including parameters and output units, and is included in exported AnnData files.

ROIs tab

Each layer has its own session-only ROIs. Enter a name or leave it blank for an automatic name. Choose Bounding box, Lasso, or Seed + radius and click Draw. Drag a box or lasso on the map; for a radius ROI, set the radius and click the seed. The radius is in µm when physical calibration is available and full-resolution pixels otherwise. Uniform image scaling preserves circular radii in original-image physical units.

For a bins layer, Seed + radius also offers All bins in a group. Select an existing group to use every native member bin's center as a seed, regardless of displayed bin aggregation or viewport. Their radius neighbourhoods are united into one ROI, retaining disconnected pieces and holes. The preview updates automatically when the group or radius changes; Preview group also rebuilds it on demand. Save ROI commits that single region. Other group members and nearby bins can belong to the ROI through the normal shape-intersection rule.

A white outline previews the draft. Save ROI commits it; Cancel discards it. Membership is computed over all native, sequenced observations whose shapes intersect or touch the region, independently of visible data and bin aggregation. Empty ROIs can still be saved and focused.

Focus fits the viewport with a small margin. Select highlights the members on the map and in embeddings; aggregated observations highlight when any member is selected. Create group makes a fixed-membership group for existing group controls and DE comparisons. Deleting an ROI also deletes its generated group and clears affected DE results. If you delete just the group, you can recreate it from the ROI. Hide outlines with the Outline checkbox. Saved shapes cannot be edited or exported in this version; redraw to replace them.

Groups tab

Define your own groups of observations from conditions on genes or metadata.

  1. Build conditions: choose a gene or numeric metadata column, an operator (>, <, >=, <=, ==) and a number, then + Add condition. From the second condition on, choose how it combines: AND or OR. Conditions are applied in order, left to right. Genes are compared on their raw counts. Clear starts over.
  2. Name the group and click ✓ Create group. To delete one, type its name and click 🗑 Delete group.
  3. The Created groups list shows each group with its size and conditions. Tick or untick groups to show or hide them on the map.

An observation in several groups is drawn with the group created last. Hiding a group on the map does not affect differential expression.

DE tab: differential expression

Finds the genes that differ between groups and/or clusters.

  1. Compare: tick groups and/or clusters. You can mix them, e.g. one group and one cluster.
  2. Against:
    • vs Rest: each ticked group or cluster against all other observations.
    • Between selected: the ticked ones against each other. Observations that belong to more than one of them are left out (the status line says how many).
  3. Resolution (bins only): test native bins or aggregated cells (summed counts) of a level. Displayed level follows the map. An aggregated cell belongs to a group or cluster when more than half of its bins do.
  4. Click ▶ Run DE. The status line shows the steps and the elapsed time.

Results appear below the map:

  • A table of genes with, per group or cluster, the log2 fold change (green up, red down) and the adjusted p-value (white when < 0.05). Click a column header to sort (again to reverse); the search box finds any gene, not only the 200 rows shown.
  • Plots of the top genes of each group or cluster: a dot plot for spots (fold change, and the share of observations expressing the gene), violin plots for bins and cells. Hover dots or violins for gene, group, adjusted p-value, fold change and expression statistics. Use the plot toolbar to pan, zoom, select, reset or save. Click a legend entry to hide or show that comparison; violin hover also reports the median and quartiles.

Method: Wilcoxon rank-sum test on log1p counts, genes detected in at least three observations, Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted p-values. Each group or cluster needs at least three observations.

Parameters tab

Extra drawing parameters of the layer: line colour, line width, fill alpha, line alpha. Colour and alpha themselves are set in the Color and General tabs.

  • Fixed value: one value for all observations, plus a value for not-sequenced ones.
  • From data: a gene or numeric column scaled between Output min and Output max; for colours, a categorical column (one colour per category). Optionally show the column in the hover.
  • The list shows the parameters added so far: ✎ to edit, ✕ to remove.

Summary panel

General Info compares the layers: number of observations, sequenced and not, % sequenced, mean UMIs (counts) per observation and UMIs per µm². Each layer then has:

  • QC: distributions of % mitochondrial reads and genes per observation, and UMIs against % mitochondrial reads and against genes.
  • Markers: the top ten marker genes of each cluster of the last clustering, with score, adjusted p-value and log fold change.

Embeddings

Each layer has a tab. Choose PCA, UMAP or t-SNE and click Run embedding. Embeddings use the same preprocessing as clustering, so a UMAP after clustering only computes the UMAP itself. For bins they use the displayed aggregation level. Select points in the embedding to highlight the same observations on the map, and the other way round.

Comparison

Compares two layers of the same tissue, e.g. pseudospots vs bins, or two segmentation methods.

  1. Sample 1 / Sample 2: the two layers. The one with larger geometries is sample 1.
  2. Match high-res geometries that are inside or overlapping each sample-1 geometry, and the Min. coverage a sample-1 geometry needs to be compared.
  3. Optionally, the clusters and cell types of each sample, to compare them too.
  4. Click Compare samples.

Results come in three tabs. Global lists summary metrics; hover ⓘ or click a metric for its explanation. Local has per-geometry metrics: show one on the map, or plot two against each other and select points to find them on the map. Genes lists per-gene agreement; click a gene to show it on both layers.

A typical analysis

  1. Color: type a gene you know to check the tissue and the data line up.
  2. Clustering: choose and run a clustering algorithm; look at the clusters on the map and their marker genes.
  3. Groups: define regions of interest, e.g. Ttr > 2.
  4. DE: compare a group with a cluster, or clusters with each other, and browse the table.
  5. Embeddings: run a UMAP and select a cluster to see where it lies in the tissue.
  6. With segmented cells, compare methods side by side, or against bins in Comparison.
